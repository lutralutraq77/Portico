## Connector revocation, restart and replay

CONN-04 runs both connector disable and connector-certificate revocation through the real controller store while the actual Linux command carries two active, distinct data streams. A separately enrolled connector B keeps its own session active on the same controller and device. Each A stream must close, stop accepting writes and join its traffic fixture; B must still echo data and remain authorized before its later explicit close.

The test saves a real hosting snapshot, then captures the last accepted lease sequences in the same transaction that revokes A. Old renewal requests are rejected while their cached leases are still within their original time bounds. Original activation requests and the saved resource revision cannot create or revive authority. The command then stops and restarts with byte-identical protected configuration. Observed authentication rejections and retries prove that the new process actually attempted to bind; an idle process cannot satisfy this check. Its old identity never obtains a waiting carrier binding or reconnects to the destination.

The workload layer is checked independently by bypassing the outer carrier with an in-memory connection that still performs actual inner TLS. A fresh server receives the original identity and the old snapshot's exact resource tuple. An observation wrapper confirms it consults the real live hosting endpoint and rejects the open before a destination connection. The saved session IDs/sequences cannot renew or activate after restart. Both old sessions remain closed without sequence/deadline extension or new session creation, even though their underlying grant and HostBinding records remain enabled. B can still refresh its own hosting state.

The destination observes exactly two A connections and one B connection; all close and all traffic, carrier and process workers join. The accepted destination handle's closed counter advances only after its Close call returns. The test does not require a closure receipt from a revoked identity: those authenticated requests may be denied, while actual socket/process closure remains independently observed. [Executable scenario](../internal/controller/connector_revocation_linux_test.go).

## Independent stream endings

PROTO-03 opens three concurrent complete workload streams through one connector runtime. Distinct position-tagged payloads span multiple DATA frames and non-aligned tails. Concurrent writes and reads must preserve every byte, its order and stream identity.

Canceling B's caller context closes only B. C remains usable while A writes a further request and half-closes. A's destination withholds its final response until it reads request EOF, then sends the request tail plus newly generated response bytes. Receiving that complete response and peer EOF proves that half-close preserved the reverse direction; an early echo cannot satisfy the oracle. C remains usable afterward, then its raw carrier is abandoned without an application FIN or workload Close call.

Each ending must close its destination and client handle. The connector records one closure receipt per session after its workers join. The runtime then shuts down with zero live streams, waiting bindings, endpoint/watch handles or relay workers. Exact destination connection and received-byte counts reject replay or extra delivery. [Executable scenario](../internal/controller/workload_protocol_test.go), [documented semantics](workload-runtime.md).
